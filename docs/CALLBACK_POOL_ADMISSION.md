# Canonical withdrawal callback admission

New deployments of `BitcoinWithdrawalEscrow` require a configured `CallbackPoolRegistry` before accepting any S/CS callback. Direct MP requests (`pool == address(0)`) and treasury requests keep their existing behavior. The new registry is separate from the deposit registry and does not grant minting or custody authority.

## Why the boundary changed

The archived escrow trusted a caller's `getFrostMultisig`, `withdrawalEscrow` and `withdrawalPrepared` getters. A counterfeit contract could repeat those public values, deposit real MP, and revert its mandatory `settleWithdrawal` callback after a Bitcoin payment. The escrow correctly rolled back its EVM accounting, but that rollback could not undo the Bitcoin payment or make the callback cooperative.

`web3/test/fixtures/LegacyCallbackEscrow.sol` preserves that baseline contract from commit `733831ec48e3df96a2356623edf17771f64ba16d`, with only its class/import paths and a test-only comment changed. `testBaselineCounterfeitPoolLocksRealMPAndRevertsWholeSettlement` executes its actual lock, bind and settlement with real MP collateral and a malicious callback. Final SPV/FROST cryptographic results are explicit unit fixtures; the test does not claim to broadcast a Bitcoin transaction.

## Admission and authorization

The concrete `SimpleMultiPoolDeployer` and `MultiPoolDeployer` record each successfully created pool's exact runtime code hash. The share-token deployers similarly record their actual creations. These mappings have no arbitrary registration setter. A user's matching getters, event, deployment JSON, or standalone `eth_call` cannot create a record.

`CallbackPoolRegistry(escrow, authorization, simpleDeployer, confederationDeployer)` permanently binds its graph and supported creation origins. One origin may be zero for a deployment that supports only the other profile. The existing escrow monetary authority authorizes the one-time `setCallbackPoolRegistry` operation. There is no independent admission administrator, removal method, or replaceable callback implementation.

Bootstrap and signers must verify the exact registry and deployer source/runtime/constructor provenance before accepting those roots. An arbitrary external contract pretending to be a registry or deployer is not a canonical root. The client's `callback_admission` reviewer supplies that verification and also qualifies the CS custodian validator. Ordinary custody/FROST and custodian approval rules remain in force.

For each callback, the registry checks the recorded pool and share runtimes, the pool's authorization and escrow, and the share token's reciprocal pool binding. `lock`, `bind`, and replacement bind check the exact stored withdrawal owner, MP token, amount, output script, local ID, pending collateral and processed/cancelled state. Bind also requires the real pool's prepared state. Signers repeat admission before monetary authorization and Bitcoin signing.

Settlement retains the mandatory callback and atomic token/share accounting. It does not catch or suppress a callback revert. Existing admission is immutable, and settlement does not require a new mutable authorization for a Bitcoin payment already admitted and signed.

## Deployment and migration

The bootstrap planner deploys and threshold-binds the registry. The legacy real-Bitcoin integration fixture also resolves its genuine simple-pool deployer through the federation factory and binds the same registry before signing. New ABI artifacts are generated by `python3 ops/generate_abis.py`.

This change affects newly built deployments. It cannot patch an already deployed immutable escrow. Existing Pending, Bound, Signing, replacement and Bitcoin-paid/EVM-unsettled obligations need an inventory and an explicit continuity plan. A legacy callback must not be silently dropped, marked Paid, refunded after signing, or retrospectively revoked to bypass its accounting. No public deployment or live-obligation migration was performed in this work.

## Evidence and limits

Local evidence is under `ops/artifacts/zkspv/evidence/`: counterfeit rejection, wrong-origin and runtime negatives, exact request checks, mixed direct-MP/S settlement, preserved S/CS fee/rounding behavior, and deployment size tests. `withdrawal-subsidy-report.json` distinguishes this callback fix from the unimplemented BTC/MP/S subsidy ledgers and their remaining gates. Address issuance, derivation verification and derived-key signing are also separate unresolved scope; no new address is Ready for funding.

The recorded regression used Solidity 0.8.30, Osaka, via-IR and optimizer runs 1. Current compiler pins and the 24,576-byte runtime and 49,152-byte initcode limits are listed in [contract build verification](CONTRACT_SIZE_AND_DEPLOYMENT.md). The complete regression runner uses private artifacts so its test linking cannot change client artifacts.
